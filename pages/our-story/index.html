Our story

A family recipe, carried on.

Chololoy® is a family-owned business committed to our family’s authentic Mexican flavors — and to carrying on the tradition our beloved brother left us.

The founder

Victor Hitchcock Medina

Victor’s passion for authentic Mexican flavors started in his hometown of León, Guanajuato. He committed himself to finding a way to share his family’s special recipes — and stumbled upon the cookbook of our great-grandmother Nemesia García, from the state of Tlaxcala. In it he found the tortilla soup he had loved as a child. It was the first flavor he wanted to share.

How the story began

Seattle

From a family kitchen to restaurant kitchens

Victor settled in Seattle, Washington in the late 1980s and dedicated himself to making the same tortilla soup in a dehydrated form that Mexican restaurants could easily use. Its quality and the authentic taste of Mexico won him the largest and most important Mexican restaurant chain in the Pacific Northwest and Florida.

Many have tried to copy the recipe; it has never been equaled. Ours is the original tortilla soup that our Mexican family has enjoyed for generations — made with 100% natural ingredients and without preservatives.

Timeline

Four generations, one recipe.

  1. Tlaxcala

    Mama Mecho — Nemesia García — keeps a family cookbook in Tlaxcala, Mexico. In it: a tortilla soup.

  2. Late 1980s

    Victor Hitchcock Medina, from León, Guanajuato, settles in Seattle and turns the recipe into a dehydrated seasoning restaurants can use.

  3. 1990

    Chololoy Food Company is founded in Seattle. Victor wins the largest Mexican restaurant chain in the Pacific Northwest.

  4. 1990s

    Almost entirely by word of mouth, Chololoy reaches hundreds of restaurants and distributors across the U.S.

  5. 2013 · 2017

    Victor’s siblings, Patrick Hitchcock Medina and Kristina Knutson, take the business on and bring it back to the U.S. as Chololoy, Inc.

  6. Today

    From tortilla soup base to everyday seasoning — in restaurant kitchens now, and soon in home kitchens.

The family today

Carried by Kristina and Patrick.

After Victor’s death, his children Kristina and Patrick have run Chololoy for two decades — keeping the recipes, the relationships with chefs, and the family kitchen alive. Every order still goes through their hands.

What’s next

Not just soup

For years Chololoy was used mainly as tortilla soup in Mexican restaurants. Then we began using it at home — and so did friends and family. Not just as soup, but as a seasoning: in pastas, seafood and modern dishes, at breakfast, lunch and dinner. We’re not changing the product. We’re expanding how it’s used.
